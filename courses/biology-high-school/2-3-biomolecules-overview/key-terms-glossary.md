# Key Terms & Glossary — 2.3 Biomolecules Overview

Every word from this lesson, including words only shown in the slide pictures. Your teacher's wording comes first. Any extra explanation starts with "Outside source:" and comes from *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*.

## Molecules and carbon

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Macromolecule** | Slide 2's heading. Outside source: a big molecule made of smaller pieces. |
| **Monomer** | Monomers + monomers = polymer. |
| **Polymer** | What monomers make when they join. |
| **Polymerization** | The label on the arrow from monomers to polymer. |
| **Valence electrons** | Carbon has four. Outside source: outer electrons an atom uses to bond. |
| **Organic** | Contains carbon. |
| **Inorganic** | Does not contain carbon. |

## Energy

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Energy** | The ability to change or move matter. |
| **Laws of thermodynamics** | The 1st and 2nd laws about energy on Slide 3. |
| **1st law of thermodynamics** | Can't destroy or create energy. It just changes forms. |
| **2nd law of thermodynamics** | Entropy always increases. |
| **Entropy** | Low entropy is highly ordered. High entropy is more disordered. |
| **Potential energy** | Chart: chemical, gravitational, electrical, nuclear, elastic. Outside source: stored energy. |
| **Kinetic energy** | Chart: electrical, light, sound, thermal, wind. Outside source: energy of motion. |
| **Chemical energy** | Potential energy; the cannon starts with it. Outside source: energy stored in bonds. |
| **Gravitational energy** | A potential energy on the chart. Outside source: stored energy from height. |
| **Electrical energy** | On both sides of the chart. Outside source: energy from electric charges. |
| **Nuclear energy** | A potential energy on the chart. Outside source: energy stored inside atoms. |
| **Elastic energy** | A potential energy on the chart. Outside source: energy in stretched things. |
| **Light energy** | A kinetic energy on the chart. |
| **Sound energy** | A kinetic energy on the chart. Outside source: energy carried by vibrations. |
| **Thermal energy** | A kinetic energy on the chart. Outside source: heat energy. |
| **Wind energy** | A kinetic energy on the chart. |
| **Heat** | The kinetic energy of molecules (cannon picture). |

## Reactions and energy graphs

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Chemical reaction** | Bonds break and/or form. The chemical itself changes. |
| **Physical change** | Changing state or being cut into pieces. Melted ice is still water. |
| **Reactants** | The substances on the left of the arrow. |
| **Products** | The substances on the right of the arrow. |
| **Coefficient** | The number in front. It tells how many of that substance are needed. |
| **Letter in parentheses** | Tells the state of matter: (g) gas, (l) liquid, (s) solid, (aq) aqueous. |
| **Aqueous (aq)** | Dissolved. |
| **Exothermic** | Releases energy as heat. Hotter than its surroundings. |
| **Endothermic** | Absorbs energy from its surroundings as heat. Cooler than its surroundings. |
| **Spontaneously** | Happening on its own. |
| **Potential energy diagram** | A graph that shows if a reaction is endothermic or exothermic. |
| **Reaction coordinate / reaction progress** | The across axis on the potential energy diagrams. |
| **Activation energy** | The blue arrow up to the hump. Outside source: energy needed to start a reaction. |

## Building and breaking

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anabolism (anabolic)** | Creating large molecules from smaller ones, using energy. |
| **Catabolism (catabolic)** | Breaking large molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy. |
| **Dehydration synthesis** | Builds up molecules by losing water molecules. |
| **Hydrolysis** | Splits a compound into pieces by adding water. |
| **Digestion** | Slide 9's label for hydrolysis. Outside source: breaking down food in your body. |
| **Starch** | Hydrolysis breaks starch into glucose. |

## ATP

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** | The "energy currency of the cell." |
| **Phosphate group** | ATP has three. Outside source: a group containing phosphorus. |
| **Ribose** | ATP's sugar. Also the sugar in RNA. |
| **Adenine** | ATP's nitrogenous base. Also a base in DNA and RNA. |
| **Nitrogenous base** | What the slide calls adenine. Outside source: a base containing nitrogen. |
| **ADP (adenosine diphosphate)** | What's left of ATP, with two phosphates. |
| **Inorganic phosphate (Pi)** | The phosphate that breaks off in ATP hydrolysis. |
| **ATP hydrolysis** | ATP + H₂O → Pi + ADP + energy. |

## Lipids

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lipid** | CHO; fatty acids and glycerol; long-term energy storage. |
| **Fatty acid** | A lipid building block, drawn as a carbon chain. |
| **Glycerol** | A lipid building block. It holds three fatty acids in a triglyceride. |
| **Triglyceride** | Glycerol plus three fatty acids. |
| **Phospholipid** | A lipid type. Phospholipids form the bilayer (cell membrane). |
| **Omega-3 phospholipid** | A phospholipid shown fitting into the cell membrane lipid bilayer. |
| **Cholesterol** | A lipid type shown on Slide 14. |
| **Phospholipid bilayer** | The cell membrane. |
| **Cell membrane** | Formed by the phospholipid bilayer. Outside source: a cell's outer layer. |
| **kcal** | Lipids: 9 per gram. Carbs: 4. Outside source: a food-label Calorie. |
| **Insulation** | A lipid job. Outside source: keeping heat in. |
| **Cell signaling** | A lipid job. Outside source: cells sending messages. |

## Carbohydrates

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** | CHO in a 1:2:1 ratio; monosaccharides; short-term energy storage. |
| **Monosaccharide** | The carb building block. Drawn as one ring. |
| **Disaccharide** | Drawn as two rings joined. |
| **Polysaccharide** | Drawn as many rings in a branching chain. |
| **Glucose** | A carb that gets converted to ATP. |
| **-ose** | The carbohydrate suffix identifier. |
| **Cell wall** | Carbs form cell walls. Outside source: a stiff layer around plant cells. |
| **Cell recognition** | A carb job. Outside source: cells identifying each other. |

## Nucleic acids

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nucleic acid** | CHONP; nucleotides; stores and carries genetic information. |
| **Nucleotide** | The building block: a phosphate, a sugar, and a base. |
| **Base (nucleobase)** | A nucleotide part: adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine, or uracil. |
| **DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)** | Stores genetic information. Double-stranded, with deoxyribose. |
| **RNA (ribonucleic acid)** | Involved with protein synthesis. Usually single-stranded, with ribose. |
| **Deoxyribose** | The sugar in DNA. |
| **Double-stranded / single-stranded** | DNA has two strands. RNA usually has one. |
| **Base pair** | Adenine with thymine, guanine with cytosine (Slide 27). |
| **Sugar phosphate backbone** | The label along the long sides of DNA. |
| **Thymine (T)** | A DNA base. Pairs with adenine. |
| **Uracil (U)** | An RNA base. |
| **Guanine (G)** | A DNA and RNA base. Pairs with cytosine. |
| **Cytosine (C)** | A DNA and RNA base. Pairs with guanine. |
| **Protein synthesis** | What RNA is involved with. Outside source: making proteins. |
| **Genetic information** | What DNA stores. The cartoon calls DNA a blueprint for a living thing. |
