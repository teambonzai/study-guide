# 2.3 Biomolecules Overview — Study Guide

**Course:** Biology (high school) · **Unit:** Unit Two: Biomolecules · **Sources:** Teacher's slides and speaker notes. No recording.

## What you'll learn

- What monomers and polymers are, and why carbon matters
- Two big rules about energy
- How a chemical change is different from a physical change
- Reactions that give off heat or take in heat
- How cells build and break molecules, and how ATP stores energy
- The four biomolecules: what they're made of, where they're found, and their jobs

## 1. Monomers, polymers, and carbon 🎯

**Macromolecules are big molecules built from small pieces joined together.** *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*

- A **monomer** is one small building block.
- A **polymer** is many monomers joined together. Monomers + monomers = polymer.
- **Carbon** has four **valence electrons** (outer electrons used for bonds). So carbon can form rings and chains. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **Organic** molecules contain carbon. **Inorganic** molecules do not.

> **Analogy:** A monomer is one train car. A polymer is the whole train.

> 📷 See **Slide 2** — loose green monomers join into one long polymer chain.

## 2. Energy and the laws of thermodynamics 🎯

**Energy is the ability to change or move matter.**

- Energy can change from one form to another.
- **1st law:** Energy can't be created or destroyed. It only changes form.
- **2nd law:** **Entropy** always increases. Entropy means disorder.
- Energy can be **potential** (stored) or **kinetic** (moving). *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*

> **Analogy:** Your room gets messy on its own. Cleaning it up takes energy.

> 📷 See **Slide 3** — neat rows of dots spread out into a messy scatter. That is entropy increasing.

---

## 3. Chemical reactions 🎯

**In a chemical reaction, bonds break or form. The chemical becomes something new.**

- A **physical change** only changes state or size. Melting ice is still H₂O.
- In 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O, **reactants** are on the left. **Products** are on the right.
- The **coefficient** is the number in front. It tells how many you need.
- Letters show the state: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

> **Why:** Boiling water is still H₂O, so it's a physical change. Splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen gas makes new chemicals. That is a chemical change.

> 📷 See **Slide 4** — the big red X over melting ice means melting is not a chemical reaction.

---

## 4. Exothermic and endothermic reactions 🎯

**Some reactions give off heat. Others take heat in.**

- **Exothermic** reactions release heat. They feel hotter than their surroundings.
- **Endothermic** reactions absorb heat. They feel cooler than their surroundings.
- The notes say exothermic reactions can happen on their own. Endothermic reactions need energy put in.
- On a **potential energy diagram**, compare products to reactants. Products lower means exothermic. Products higher means endothermic.

> **Analogy:** **Exo** sounds like **exit**, so heat leaves. **Endo** sounds like **enter**, so heat comes in.

> 📷 See **Slide 7** — two energy graphs with red "energy released" and "energy absorbed" arrows. ⛔ Skip the blue activation energy arrow.

## 5. Building and breaking molecules 🎯

**Building big molecules uses energy. Breaking them apart releases energy.**

- **Anabolism** builds large molecules from small ones. It uses energy.
- **Catabolism** breaks large molecules into small ones. It releases energy.
- **Dehydration synthesis** builds molecules by removing water.
- **Hydrolysis** splits molecules by adding water. Example: starch breaks into glucose.

> **Why:** The names give it away. "De-hydration" means losing water. "Hydro-lysis" means splitting with water. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*

> 📷 See **Slide 9** — water leaves when two rings join. Water is added back to split them (digestion).

## 6. ATP, the cell's energy currency 🎯

**ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the "energy currency of the cell."**

- ATP has three phosphate groups, one ribose sugar, and one adenine (a base with nitrogen).
- **ATP hydrolysis:** ATP + water → ADP + Pi + energy.
- **ADP** has two phosphates. **Pi** is the one phosphate that broke off.
- Turning ADP + Pi back into ATP takes energy. Water comes out.

> **Analogy:** ATP is a charged battery. Using it leaves a dead battery (ADP + Pi). Recharging takes energy.

> 📷 See **Slide 13** — the cycle between the charged battery (ATP) and the dead battery (ADP + Pi).

---

## 7. Lipids 🎯

**Lipids store energy for the long term.**

- **Made of:** carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen (CHO).
- **Building blocks:** fatty acids and glycerol.
- **Found in:** oils, butter, dairy, and waxes.
- **Job:** long-term energy storage. One gram holds 9 **kcal** (a unit of food energy).
- Lipids form the **phospholipid bilayer** (the cell membrane). They also keep heat in and help cells send signals.

> **Why:** A gram of lipid holds 9 kcal. A gram of carbohydrate holds only 4. So lipids pack more energy.

> 📷 See **Slide 14** — a blue glycerol piece with fatty acid chains attached.

---

## 8. Carbohydrates 🎯

**Carbohydrates ("carbs") store energy for the short term.**

- **Made of:** carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in a 1:2:1 ratio.
- **Building blocks:** **monosaccharides** (single sugars). Their names end in **-ose**, like glucose.
- **Found in:** sugar, bread, pasta, rice, and fruits.
- **Job:** short-term energy storage. One gram holds 4 kcal.
- Carbs also form cell walls and help cells recognize each other.

> **Analogy:** Carbs are like cash in your pocket, quick to spend. Lipids are like savings in the bank.

> 📷 See **Slide 18** — one sugar ring, two rings joined, and a big branched chain of many rings.

---

## 9. Proteins 🎯

**Proteins give structure and form enzymes in your cells.**

- **Made of:** carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and sometimes sulfur (CHON(S)).
- **Building blocks:** amino acids. Enzyme names end in **-ase**.
- **Found in:** meat, eggs, beans, tofu, dairy, and nuts.
- **Job:** a backup energy source and structural support.
- Proteins form enzymes. They also help with cell transport, signaling, and immune defense.

> **Why:** An **enzyme** is a protein that speeds up a reaction. Enzyme names usually end in -ase. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*

> 📷 See **Slide 22** — a protein is a long chain of beads. Each bead is an amino acid.

---

## 10. Nucleic acids 🎯

**Nucleic acids store and carry genetic information.**

- **Made of:** carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and phosphorus (CHONP).
- **Building blocks:** nucleotides.
- **Found in:** any living cells, like meats, plants, and mushrooms. They are there as DNA and RNA.
- **Job:** store, code, and carry genetic information. **DNA** stores it. **RNA** helps make proteins.

> **Analogy:** DNA is like a blueprint, the plan for building a living thing.

> 📷 See **Slide 26** — one nucleotide: a phosphate, a sugar, and a base joined together.

---

## Compare the four biomolecules

**Same pattern for each: what it's made of, its building block, and its job.**

| Biomolecule | Building block | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **Lipids** (CHO) | Fatty acids & glycerol | Long-term energy, 9 kcal/g |
| **Carbohydrates** (CHO, 1:2:1) | Monosaccharides | Short-term energy, 4 kcal/g |
| **Proteins** (CHON(S)) | Amino acids | Structure and enzymes |
| **Nucleic acids** (CHONP) | Nucleotides | Store genetic information |

## Quick review

- Monomers join to make polymers.
- Organic means it contains carbon.
- Energy can't be created or destroyed. Entropy always increases.
- Exothermic releases heat. Endothermic absorbs heat.
- Dehydration synthesis builds (water out). Hydrolysis breaks (water in).
- ATP + water → ADP + Pi + energy.
- Lipids: 9 kcal/g, long-term. Carbs: 4 kcal/g, short-term.
- Proteins come from amino acids. Nucleic acids come from nucleotides.
