## A few clearer versions

Learn the slides as written. These notes explain a few ideas more clearly. They come from other books, so each one starts with **Outside source:**.

- **Electrical energy on both sides of the chart** *(Slide 3)*: **Outside source:** Electrical energy can be stored or moving. Static charge on a rubbed balloon is stored. Electricity flowing through a lamp cord is moving. *(OpenStax Physics 2e)*
- **"Entropy always increases"** *(Slide 3)*: **Outside source:** This is true for the universe as a whole. Living things can build order, but they use energy to do it. It's like cleaning your room: it gets neater, but you get hot and tired. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **Exothermic reactions "can happen on their own"** *(Slide 5 notes)*: **Outside source:** Some exothermic reactions still need a push to start. That push is activation energy (Slide 7). Burning wood gives off heat, but it needs a match first. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **The melting ice cube as "endothermic"** *(Slide 5)*: **Outside source:** The ice picture shows heat flowing in. Melting is still a physical change, not a chemical reaction (Slide 4). A melting popsicle is still the same stuff. *(OpenStax Chemistry 2e)*
- **Fatty acids and glycerol as "monomers"** *(Slide 14)*: **Outside source:** Lipids aren't true polymers. A polymer is a long chain of repeating monomers, like beads on a necklace. A triglyceride is just one glycerol with three fatty acids (Slide 14). *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **"CHO in a 1:2:1 ratio"** *(Slide 18)*: **Outside source:** This ratio fits simple sugars best. Glucose (Slide 18) has 6 carbons, 12 hydrogens, and 6 oxygens. That's 1:2:1, like a recipe of 1 cup, 2 cups, 1 cup. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **Carbs "form cell walls"** *(Slide 20)*: **Outside source:** Plant cells have cell walls. Animal cells do not. The crunch in celery comes from its cell walls. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **"-ase" for proteins** *(Slide 22)*: **Outside source:** -ase really marks enzymes, which are one kind of protein. Not every protein ends in -ase. Lactase and lipase end in -ase, but actin and myosin (Slide 24) don't. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- **The sugar labeled "ribose"** *(Slide 26)*: **Outside source:** Ribose is the sugar in RNA. DNA nucleotides have deoxyribose instead (Slide 28). "Deoxy" means one oxygen is missing. Compare the Slide 28 BONUS picture: H instead of OH. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
