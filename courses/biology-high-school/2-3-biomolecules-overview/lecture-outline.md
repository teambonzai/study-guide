## 1. Monomers, polymers, and carbon
- Macromolecules: monomers + monomers = polymer! *(Slide 2)*
- The arrow from monomers to polymer is labeled polymerization.
- Carbon has four valence electrons.
- Carbon forms rings, chains, and bonds to other carbon and other elements.
- Organic contains carbon. Inorganic does not.
- Pictures: a long carbon chain (a), a small molecule (b), a ring (c).

## 2. Energy and the laws of thermodynamics
- Energy is the ability to change or move matter. It can convert forms. *(Slide 3)*
- Potential: chemical, gravitational, electrical, nuclear, elastic.
- Kinetic: electrical, light, sound, thermal, wind.
- 1st law: you can't destroy or create energy. It just changes forms.
- Cannon: chemical → kinetic + potential → less kinetic + more potential → heat.
- Heat = kinetic energy of molecules.
- 2nd law: entropy always increases.
- Dots: low entropy (highly ordered) → high entropy (more disordered).

## 3. Chemical reactions
- Chemical reaction: bonds break and/or form. The chemical itself changes. *(Slide 4)*
- Physical change: changing state or being cut into pieces.
- Melting, boiling, and freezing are NOT chemical reactions. Melted ice is still water.
- Boiled water is still H₂O, just a gas.
- Breaking water into hydrogen and oxygen gas is a chemical change.
- 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O: reactants on the left, products on the right.
- Coefficient = number in front = how many are needed (2 H₂, 1 O₂, 2 H₂O).
- Letters in parentheses: (g) gas, (l) liquid, (s) solid, (aq) aqueous (dissolved). O₂ (g) = oxygen gas.
- Red X over melting ice: not a chemical reaction.

## 4. Exothermic and endothermic reactions
- Exothermic releases energy as heat. Hotter than surroundings (fire). *(Slide 5)*
- Endothermic absorbs energy from surroundings as heat. Cooler than surroundings (ice cube).
- Exothermic can happen on its own (spontaneously).
- Endothermic can't happen without an input of energy.

## 5. Potential energy diagrams
- Axes: potential energy (up) and reaction coordinate or reaction progress (across). *(Slides 6–7)*
- Compare the energy level of products versus reactants.
- Products lower = energy released = exothermic. *(Slide 7)*
- Products higher = energy absorbed = endothermic.
- Blue arrow = activation energy, from reactants to the top of the hump.
- Outside source: activation energy is the energy needed to start a reaction. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*
- Teacher's note on Slide 7: “We will talk more about the ‘activation energy’ concept in a future unit, for now we are just focusing on the exo/endo thermic ideas (energy releasing/absorbing)”

## 6. Building and breaking molecules
- Anabolism: creates large molecules from smaller ones, using energy. *(Slide 8)*
- Catabolism: breaks large molecules into smaller ones, releasing energy.
- Picture: anabolic = small assembled into large, energy required.
- Picture: catabolic = large broken into small, energy released.
- Dehydration synthesis: builds molecules by losing water. *(Slide 9)*
- Hydrolysis: splits a compound by adding water. Breaks polymers, like starch into glucose.
- Picture: OH + H leave as H₂O when rings join. Water splits them back.
- Hydrolysis is labeled "digestion."

## 7. ATP, the cell's energy currency
- ATP = adenosine triphosphate, the "energy currency of the cell." *(Slide 10)*
- Parts: three phosphate groups, one ribose sugar, one adenine (nitrogenous base).
- ATP + H₂O → Pi (inorganic phosphate) + ADP (adenosine diphosphate) + energy. *(Slide 12)*
- Cycle: ATP = charged battery. Water in, releases energy. *(Slide 13)*
- ADP + Pi = dead battery. Rebuilding ATP requires energy, and water comes out.

## 8. Lipids
- Made of CHO. Building blocks: fatty acids and glycerol. *(Slide 14)*
- Picture: phospholipid, triglyceride, cholesterol; phospholipid bilayer (cell membrane).
- Triglyceride = glycerol + three fatty acids (carbon chains).
- Found in oils, butter, dairy, and waxes. Photos: butter, milk. *(Slide 15)*
- Job: long-term energy storage, 9 kcal per gram. *(Slide 16)*
- Cell jobs: phospholipid bilayer (membrane), stores energy, insulation, cell signaling.
- Picture: omega-3 phospholipid, cell membrane lipid bilayer, phospholipid, protein, cell.

## 9. Carbohydrates
- Made of CHO in a 1:2:1 ratio. Building blocks: monosaccharides. *(Slide 18)*
- Suffix identifier: -ose.
- Picture: the structure of glucose (chain, ring, 3-D model).
- Picture: monosaccharide (1 ring), disaccharide (2), polysaccharide (many, branched).
- Found in sugar, bread, pasta, rice, and fruits. Photos: rice, candy. *(Slide 19)*
- Job: short-term energy storage, 4 kcal per gram. *(Slide 20)*
- Cell jobs: forms cell walls, provides energy, cell recognition.
- Picture: glucose gets converted to ATP (energy). Photo: fruit.

## 10. Proteins
- Made of CHON(S). Building blocks: amino acids. Suffix identifier: -ase. *(Slide 22)*
- Amino acid picture: center C, H, amine group, carboxyl group, variable group (R).
- Chain picture: amino acids, peptide bond, N-terminus and C-terminus ends.
- Found in meat, eggs, beans, tofu, dairy, and nuts. *(Slide 23)*
- Job: secondary energy source, structural support. *(Slide 24)*
- Cell jobs: enzymes, cell transport, structural support, signaling, immune defense.
- Digestive enzymes for bread: amylase, sucrase-isomaltase, maltase, lactase.
- For meat: pepsin, trypsin, peptidase. For oil: lipase.
- Muscle picture: myofiber, myofibril, actin, troponin, tropomyosin, myosin.

## 11. Nucleic acids
- Made of CHONP. Building blocks: nucleotides. *(Slide 26)*
- Nucleotide picture: phosphate, ribose (sugar), base.
- RNA vs DNA picture: RNA bases C, G, A, U. DNA bases C, G, A, T.
- Picture labels: nucleobases, base pair, helix of sugar-phosphates.
- Found in any living cells (meats, plants, mushrooms) as DNA & RNA. *(Slide 27)*
- Picture: base pairs (adenine–thymine, guanine–cytosine), sugar phosphate backbone.
- Job: information storage; codes and transports genetic information. *(Slide 28)*
- DNA stores genetic information. RNA is involved with protein synthesis.
- DNA: double-stranded, deoxyribose, thymine. RNA: usually single-stranded, ribose, uracil.
- Slide 28 picture label: "single nucleobase."
- BONUS picture: deoxyribose (H) vs ribose (OH).
- Cartoon: "A DNA strand like me is a blueprint for building a living thing!" *(Slide 29)*

## Compare the four biomolecules
- Lipids: CHO · fatty acids & glycerol · long-term energy, 9 kcal/g.
- Carbs: CHO 1:2:1 · monosaccharides · short-term energy, 4 kcal/g.
- Proteins: CHON(S) · amino acids · secondary energy, structural support, enzymes.
- Nucleic acids: CHONP · nucleotides · genetic information.
