# Key Terms — 2.5 Proteins & Nucleic Acids

The words in this lesson. Each meaning starts with your teacher's wording. Anything after
**Outside source:** is an extra explanation that is not from your teacher.

## Protein building blocks

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Protein** | Slide 2: 1+ polypeptides with a 3D structure. It is functional. |
| **Functional** | Slide 2 says a protein is functional. **Outside source:** Able to do its job. Example: a phone that turns on and makes calls is functional. *(Merriam-Webster Dictionary)* |
| **Regulate** | A protein function on Slide 2. **Outside source:** To control how something works. Example: a thermostat regulates a room's heat. *(Merriam-Webster Dictionary)* |
| **Transport** | A protein function on Slide 2. **Outside source:** To carry things from place to place. Example: a school bus transports students. *(Merriam-Webster Dictionary)* |
| **Structure** | A protein function on Slide 2. The slide also says "STRUCTURE = FUNCTION!" |
| **Enzyme** | Listed on Slide 2 under "Control reaction rates." **Outside source:** A protein that speeds up a reaction. Example: an enzyme in saliva starts breaking down bread. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Monomer** | Slide 2: amino acids. Slide 11: nucleotides. **Outside source:** One small building block that links with others. Example: one Lego brick. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Amino acid** | The monomer of proteins. Slide 2: about 20 different kinds. |
| **Amino group** | Labeled in the Slide 2 amino acid picture: the N with two H's. |
| **Carboxyl group** | Labeled in the Slide 2 amino acid picture: the C with an O and an OH. |
| **Side chain (R-group)** | Slide 2: "R-group / side chain = differs!" It is different in each amino acid. |
| **Nonpolar side chain** | A group on the Slide 2 chart, with 9 amino acids. Slide 8: nonpolar amino acids gather in the center. **Outside source:** Nonpolar things don't mix with water. Example: oil floats on water. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Polar side chain** | A group on the Slide 2 chart, with 6 amino acids. Slide 8: polar amino acids stay outside. **Outside source:** Polar things mix with water. Example: sugar dissolves in tea. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Electrically charged side chain** | A group on the Slide 2 chart: 5 amino acids, split into acidic and basic. |
| **Acidic** | The charged group on Slide 2 with aspartate and glutamate. |
| **Basic** | The charged group on Slide 2 with lysine, arginine, and histidine. |
| **Peptide** | Slide 2: about 2–50 amino acids. |
| **Polypeptide** | Slide 2: about 51 or more amino acids. |

## Levels of protein structure

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Primary structure** | Slide 4 notes: the sequence (order) of amino acids in a polypeptide chain. |
| **Peptide bond** | Slide 4: covalent, formed via dehydration synthesis. It joins the amino acids. |
| **Covalent** | Slide 4: the kind of bond a peptide bond is. **Outside source:** A strong bond where atoms share electrons. Example: the bonds inside a water molecule. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Dehydration synthesis** | Slide 4: how peptide bonds form. The chart shows water (H₂O) given off. |
| **N-terminus** | Labeled on Slide 4 at one end of the chain. |
| **C-terminus** | Labeled on Slide 4 at the other end of the chain. |
| **Amino end and carboxyl end** | Labeled on the Slide 4 chart at the two ends of the joined amino acids. |
| **Secondary structure** | Slide 5: most common are the α helix and β pleated sheet. Caused by hydrogen bonds. |
| **α helix (alpha helix)** | Slide 5: a secondary structure, drawn as a coil. |
| **β pleated sheet (beta pleated sheet)** | Slide 5: a secondary structure, drawn as a zigzag. |
| **Hydrogen bond** | Slide 5: causes secondary structure. Also on Slides 6, 12, and 13. **Outside source:** A weak pull between atoms. Example: it pulls water molecules together into drops. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Backbone** | Slide 5 notes: the polypeptide chain apart from the R groups. |
| **Carbonyl O** | Slide 5 notes: the O of one amino acid in a hydrogen bond. **Outside source:** The O in a C=O group. Example: nail polish remover also has a C=O group. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Amino H** | Slide 5 notes: the H of another amino acid in that hydrogen bond. **Outside source:** The H in an N–H group. Example: the N with two H's at the left of the Slide 2 amino acid drawing. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Tertiary structure** | Slide 6: 3D shape, due to interactions between amino acid side chains. |
| **Ionic bond** | Labeled on Slide 6 as a side-chain interaction. **Outside source:** A pull between a plus and a minus charge. Example: table salt is held together this way. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Disulfide bridge** | Labeled on Slide 6 as a side-chain interaction, drawn as S–S. **Outside source:** A link between two sulfur atoms, like a staple. Example: links like these help shape your hair. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Hydrophobic interaction** | Labeled on Slide 6 as a side-chain interaction. |
| **Hydrophobic** | Used in "hydrophobic interaction" and "hydrophobic effect" (Slides 6–8). **Outside source:** Water-fearing. Hydrophobic things clump together away from water. Example: oil drops in salad dressing. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Hydrophobic effect** | Title of Slides 7–8. It minimizes the hydration layer and helps weak interactions between residues. |
| **Hydration layer** | Labeled on Slide 8: the blue layer around the chain. **Outside source:** Hydration means water, so it is the water around the chain. Example: the thin coat of water on a washed grape. *(Merriam-Webster Dictionary)* |
| **Protein folding** | Labeled on Slide 8: the arrow from unfolded to folded polypeptide. |
| **Residue** | Slide 8: "amino acid residues." **Outside source:** One amino acid inside a chain. Example: one bead on a necklace. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Quaternary structure** | Slide 9: 2+ subunits. "*NOT ALL PROTEINS HAVE THIS!!!!" |
| **Subunit** | Slide 9: quaternary structure has 2+ subunits. Slide 6 notes: two or more polypeptide chains. |
| **Dimer** | A Slide 9 box, shown as a homodimer and a heterodimer. |
| **Homodimer** | Slide 9: a dimer drawn with 2 matching shapes. |
| **Heterodimer** | Slide 9: a dimer drawn with 2 different shapes. |
| **Trimer** | A Slide 9 box: "e.g. Collagen." |
| **Tetramer** | A Slide 9 box: "e.g. Hemoglobin (alpha-2-beta-2 tetramer)." |
| **Collagen** | Slide 9: the trimer example. **Outside source:** A protein that makes skin, bones, and tendons strong. Example: gelatin in gummy candy comes from collagen. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Hemoglobin** | Slide 9: the tetramer example, an "alpha-2-beta-2 tetramer." **Outside source:** A protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Example: it is why blood looks red. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Antibody** | Slide 10 sample text: "Antibodies are a useful tool." **Outside source:** A protein that helps your body fight germs. Example: vaccines teach your body to make antibodies. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |

## Nucleic acids & DNA

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nucleic acid** | Slide 11: stores and transmits hereditary info, and encodes protein products. Examples: DNA, RNA. |
| **Hereditary information** | Slide 11: what nucleic acids store and transmit. **Outside source:** Information passed from parents to children. Example: eye color. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Encode** | Slide 11: nucleic acids "encode protein products." **Outside source:** To hold the code for something. Example: a recipe holds the code for a cake. *(Merriam-Webster Dictionary)* |
| **Nucleotide** | Slide 11: the monomer of nucleic acids. Each has a 5-carbon sugar, phosphate group, and nitrogenous base. |
| **5-carbon sugar** | Slide 11: one part of each nucleotide. |
| **Phosphate group** | Slide 11: one part of each nucleotide. Drawn as P on Slides 12 and 13. |
| **Nitrogenous base** | Slide 11: one part of each nucleotide. DNA: A, T, C, G. RNA: A, U, C, G. |
| **Nucleobases** | Slide 14's label for thymine, adenine, cytosine, guanine, and uracil. |
| **Adenine (A)** | Slide 11: a purine base. Slide 14: in DNA and RNA. |
| **Guanine (G)** | Slide 11: a purine base. Slide 14: in DNA and RNA. |
| **Cytosine (C)** | Slide 11: a pyrimidine base. Slide 14: in DNA and RNA. |
| **Thymine (T)** | Slide 11: a pyrimidine base (in DNA). Slide 14: DNA only. |
| **Uracil (U)** | Slide 11: "uracil (in RNA)." Slide 14: RNA only. |
| **Purine bases** | Labeled on Slide 11 as the purine bases: adenine and guanine. |
| **Pyrimidine bases** | Labeled on Slide 11 as the pyrimidine bases: cytosine and thymine (in DNA). |
| **Deoxyribose** | Labeled on Slide 11. Slide 13 key: S = deoxyribose (sugar). Slide 14: by DNA. |
| **Ribose** | Slide 11: "ribose (in RNA)." Slide 14: by RNA. |
| **DNA** | Slide 14: deoxyribonucleic acid. Slide 11: bases A, T, C, G. Drawn as two twisted strands. |
| **RNA** | Slide 14: ribonucleic acid. Slide 11: bases A, U, C, G. Drawn as one strand. |
| **Double helix** | Labeled on Slide 12 for DNA's twisted shape. |
| **Sugar-phosphate backbone** | Slide 12 notes: the sugars and phosphates on the outside of the helix. |
| **Base pair** | Labeled on Slide 13. Slide 12 notes: bases in a pair are bound by hydrogen bonds. |
| **Complementary base pairing** | Labeled on Slides 12 and 13. The pictures pair A with T and C with G. |
| **5′ end and 3′ end** | Labeled on Slides 11 and 13 at the ends of a strand. **Outside source:** Say "five prime" and "three prime." *(OpenStax Biology 2e)* |
| **Antiparallel** | Slide 12 notes: the two strands run in opposite directions. It is important for copying DNA. |
