## 1. Title *(Slide 1)*
- 2.5: Proteins & Nucleic Acids, from Unit Two: Biomolecules.

## 2. Proteins *(Slide 2)*
- Jobs: regulate, transport, structure, control reaction rates (enzymes).
- STRUCTURE = FUNCTION! Shape decides the job.
- Monomer = amino acid. About 20 different kinds.
- Amino acid parts: amino group, carboxyl group, side chain. Green boxes cover these labels on the slide.
- The R-group / side chain differs in each amino acid.
- Nonpolar (9): Gly G, Ala A, Val V, Leu L, Ile I, Met M, Phe F, Trp W, Pro P.
- Polar (6): Ser S, Thr T, Cys C, Tyr Y, Asn N, Gln Q.
- Electrically charged (5): acidic Asp D, Glu E; basic Lys K, Arg R, His H.
- Each amino acid has a name, one-letter code, and three-letter code (Glycine, G, Gly).
- Peptide ≈ 2–50 amino acids. Polypeptide ≈ 51+ amino acids ("aa").
- Protein = 1+ polypeptides, 3D structure, functional.
- Bottom strip: amino acid → peptide → protein.

## 3. Levels of protein structure *(Slide 3)*
- Primary (amino acid chain) → secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet) → tertiary (one folded chain) → quaternary (chains packed together).
- All proteins have levels 1–3. Level 4 only with 2+ chains (Slide 6 notes).

## 4. Primary structure *(Slide 4)*
- The order (sequence) of amino acids in a chain.
- Peptide bonds join them. Covalent. Formed by dehydration synthesis (water given off).
- Chain picture ends: N-terminus and C-terminus. Chart ends: amino end and carboxyl end.
- Picture: Leu–Phe–Ser–Cys ("Set" on the slide), with a peptide bond.
- Chart: aa₁ + aa₂ + aa₃ give 2 peptide bonds + 2 H₂O.
- Notes: the gene's DNA sets the order (or part of it, for multi-subunit proteins). A DNA change can change the order.
- Notes: one changed amino acid can change the protein's shape and job.

## 5. Secondary structure *(Slide 5)*
- Most common: α helix (coil) and β pleated sheet (zigzag).
- Caused by hydrogen bonds!
- Notes: pulls between backbone atoms. The backbone is the chain without the R-groups.
- Notes: hydrogen bonds link the carbonyl O (C=O) of one amino acid to the amino H (N–H) of another.
- Picture caption: beta sheet = two chains side by side; alpha helix = coiling; red dots = hydrogen bonds.

## 6. Tertiary structure *(Slide 6)*
- The whole 3D shape of one chain.
- Due to interactions between amino acid side chains.
- Ionic bond: drawn with a + next to a −.
- Hydrogen bond: drawn as OH linked to O between two side chains.
- Disulfide bridge: drawn as S–S.
- Hydrophobic interaction: two side chains meeting in a pink box.
- Full-size picture: "Types of side chain interactions" → "Overall 3D shape (3° Structure)."
- Notes: all proteins have 1°, 2°, and 3°. 4° only with 2+ chains.

## 7. Hydrophobic effect, part 1 *(Slide 7)*
- Unfolded chain in water. Yellow = polar amino acids. Red = nonpolar amino acids.
- Ends marked NH₃⁺ and COO⁻. A green box covers the folded result.

## 8. Hydrophobic effect, part 2 *(Slide 8)*
- Unfolded polypeptide → protein folding → folded polypeptide.
- Nonpolar amino acids gather in the center. Polar ones stay outside.
- Hydration layer: the blue layer drawn around the chain.
- Hydrophobic effects shrink the hydration layer and help weak pulls between amino acid residues.

## 9. Quaternary structure *(Slide 9)*
- 2+ subunits (chains) fit together.
- *NOT ALL PROTEINS HAVE THIS!!!!
- Dimer (2): homodimer (matching) and heterodimer (different).
- Trimer (3): e.g. collagen.
- Tetramer (4): e.g. hemoglobin, an alpha-2-beta-2 tetramer.

## 10. Organization levels summary *(Slide 10)*
- Alphabets (letters) = primary · words = secondary · sentences = tertiary · paragraphs = quaternary.
- Sample boxes: letters (A, U, V, R, S, O, T, P), words (Antibodies, Useful, Science…), three short sentences, one paragraph.
- The paragraph says antibodies are a useful research tool.
- It names Western Blot, immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, immunoprecipitation, and flow cytometry.
- Pictures: amino acid dots, pleated sheet and alpha helix, one folded chain, two chains.
- Slide typo: "Quanternary."

## 11. Nucleic acids *(Slide 11)*
- Jobs: store and transmit hereditary info. Encode protein products.
- Examples: DNA, RNA. Monomer = nucleotide.
- Nucleotide = 5-carbon sugar + phosphate group + nitrogenous base.
- DNA: A, T, C, G · RNA: A, U, C, G.
- Drawing: phosphate, deoxyribose, 5′ end, 3′ end.
- Adenine and guanine = purine bases. Cytosine and thymine (in DNA) = pyrimidine bases.
- Box: ribose (in RNA) and uracil (in RNA).
- Small drawing: phosphate, ribose, base. A green box covers a small corner.

## 12. DNA structure *(Slide 12)*
- Double helix. Picture labels: backbone, complementary base pairing.
- Notes: sugars and phosphates on the outside = sugar-phosphate backbone.
- Notes: bases reach inside in pairs, like staircase steps, held by hydrogen bonds.
- A pairs with T (2 dashed lines). C pairs with G (3 dashed lines).
- Notes: strands run in opposite directions. 5′ end pairs with 3′ end.
- Notes: this is antiparallel, and it matters for copying DNA.

## 13. Additional helpful illustrations *(Slide 13)*
- Top left: S and P backbone, pairs A–T, T–A, G–C, C–G, dotted hydrogen bonds, 5′ and 3′ ends.
- Key: A adenine, C cytosine, G guanine, T thymine, P phosphate, S deoxyribose (sugar).
- Middle: complementary base pairing, 5′→3′ arrows in opposite directions, hydrogen bonds.
- Right: sugar phosphate backbones, base pairs, hydrogen bonds, one base pair, one nucleotide, a double helix.
- Slide typo: "&mages" means "Images."

## 14. DNA vs. RNA *(Slide 14)*
- Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA): thymine, deoxyribose, two strands.
- Ribonucleic acid (RNA): uracil, ribose, one strand.
- Both have adenine, cytosine, and guanine (nucleobases).
- Green boxes cover the labels above each name.
