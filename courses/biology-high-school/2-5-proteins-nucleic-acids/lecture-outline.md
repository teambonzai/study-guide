# 2.5 Proteins & Nucleic Acids — Lesson Outline

One short section per slide. There is no recording, so this follows the slides.
**🎯 = core idea · ⛔ = extra detail (don't memorize)**

## 1. Title *(Slide 1)*
- 2.5: Proteins & Nucleic Acids, from Unit Two: Biomolecules.

## 2. Proteins 🎯 *(Slide 2)*
- Jobs: regulate, transport, structure, control reaction rates (enzymes). STRUCTURE = FUNCTION!
- Monomer = amino acid. About 20 kinds. The side chain (R-group) is the part that differs.
- Peptide = about 2–50 amino acids. Polypeptide = about 51+. Protein = working 3D shape.

## 3. Levels of protein structure 🎯 *(Slide 3)*
- Primary → secondary → tertiary → quaternary.
- Every protein has the first three levels.

## 4. Primary structure 🎯 *(Slide 4)*
- The order of amino acids in a chain.
- Peptide bonds join them. They are covalent and form by dehydration synthesis (water given off).
- A gene's DNA sets the order. One change can change shape and job.

## 5. Secondary structure 🎯 *(Slide 5)*
- Small folds: the α helix (coil) and the β pleated sheet (zigzag).
- Held by hydrogen bonds along the backbone, not the side chains.

## 6. Tertiary structure 🎯 *(Slide 6)*
- The whole 3D shape of one chain.
- Side chains stick to each other in a few ways. ⛔ The names of each link.

## 7. Hydrophobic effect, part 1 🎯 *(Slide 7)*
- An unfolded chain in water. Polar (yellow) and nonpolar (red) amino acids are mixed.

## 8. Hydrophobic effect, part 2 🎯 *(Slide 8)*
- After folding, nonpolar amino acids sit in the center. Polar ones stay outside.
- Folding shrinks the hydration layer (the water around the chain).

## 9. Quaternary structure 🎯 *(Slide 9)*
- 2 or more subunits (chains) fit together.
- NOT ALL PROTEINS HAVE THIS!
- ⛔ Dimer, trimer, and tetramer examples.

## 10. Organization levels summary 🎯 *(Slide 10)*
- Letters = primary · words = secondary · sentences = tertiary · paragraphs = quaternary.

## 11. Nucleic acids 🎯 *(Slide 11)*
- Jobs: store and pass on hereditary info. Hold the code for proteins.
- Examples: DNA, RNA. Monomer = nucleotide (5-carbon sugar + phosphate group + nitrogenous base).
- DNA: A, T, C, G · RNA: A, U, C, G.

## 12. DNA structure 🎯 *(Slide 12)*
- Double helix. Sugar-phosphate backbone outside, base pairs inside.
- Hydrogen bonds hold the pairs: A with T, C with G.
- Antiparallel: the strands run in opposite directions. This matters for copying DNA.

## 13. Extra illustrations ⛔ *(Slide 13)*
- More drawings of the backbone, base pairs, and hydrogen bonds.

## 14. DNA vs. RNA 🎯 *(Slide 14)*
- DNA has thymine and deoxyribose. RNA has uracil and ribose.
- Both have adenine, cytosine, and guanine.
