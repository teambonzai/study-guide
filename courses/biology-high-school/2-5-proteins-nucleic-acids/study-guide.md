## 1. Proteins and their jobs 🎯

**Proteins do many jobs, and a protein's shape decides its job.**

- Proteins **regulate**, **transport**, and give **structure**.
- Some proteins **control reaction rates** (how fast reactions go).
- Proteins that control reaction rates are called **enzymes**.
- **STRUCTURE = FUNCTION!** A protein's shape decides the job it can do.

> **Analogy:** A key opens a lock because of its shape. Bend the key, and it stops working.

> 📷 See **Slide 2** — "STRUCTURE = FUNCTION!" is written in pink on the left.

---

## 2. Protein building blocks 🎯

**Proteins are built from small pieces called amino acids.**

- A **monomer** is one building block. The monomer of proteins is the **amino acid**.
- There are about **20** different amino acids.
- Each amino acid has a **side chain**, also called the **R-group**. It is different in each one.
- A **peptide** is a short chain of about 2–50 amino acids. A **polypeptide** is about 51 or more. A **protein** is 1 or more polypeptides with a working 3D shape.

> **Analogy:** Amino acids are like beads. String beads together, and you get a chain.

> 📷 See **Slide 2** — the bottom strip shows one amino acid, then a peptide, then a folded protein.

---

## 3. The four levels of protein structure 🎯

**A protein's shape is built in four levels. Each level builds on the one before.**

- In order, the levels are **primary**, **secondary**, **tertiary**, and **quaternary**.
- Every protein has the first three levels.
- Only some proteins have the fourth level.

> **Analogy:** Think of writing. Letters make words. Words make sentences. Sentences make paragraphs. The four levels stack up the same way. *(Slide 10)*

> 📷 See **Slide 3** — follow the arrows. First a bead chain. Then a coil and a wavy sheet. Then one folded blob. Last, chains packed together.

---

## 4. Level 1 — Primary structure 🎯

**Primary structure is the order of amino acids in the chain.**

- **What it is:** amino acids lined up in a certain order.
- **Key fact:** **peptide bonds** join the amino acids. A peptide bond is **covalent**, a strong bond where atoms share electrons *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*.
- Peptide bonds form by **dehydration synthesis**. Two pieces join, and water is given off.
- A gene's **DNA** sets the order. Changing one amino acid can change its shape and job.

> **Analogy:** Level 1 is like **letters**. Order matters. Change one letter, and "cat" becomes "cut."

> 📷 See **Slide 4** — the chart on the right shows three amino acids joining, with water given off.

---

## 5. Level 2 — Secondary structure 🎯

**Secondary structure is small folds in the chain, like coils and zigzags.**

- **What it is:** small folded shapes. The **α helix** (alpha helix) is a coil, like a spring.
- The **β pleated sheet** (beta pleated sheet) is a zigzag, like a folded paper fan.
- **Key fact:** **hydrogen bonds** hold these shapes. A hydrogen bond is a weak pull between atoms *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*.
- They form along the **backbone**: the chain itself, not the side chains.

> **Analogy:** Level 2 is like **words**. Letters group into words. The chain groups into coils and sheets.

> 📷 See **Slide 5** — dotted lines on the coil and the zigzag show the hydrogen bonds.

---

## 6. Level 3 — Tertiary structure 🎯

**Tertiary structure is the whole 3D shape of one folded chain.**

- **What it is:** the full 3D shape of one polypeptide.
- **Key fact:** side chains pull on each other to make this shape.
- Side chains stick to each other in a few different ways.
- ⛔ The slide names each kind of link (ionic bond, hydrogen bond, disulfide bridge, hydrophobic interaction). You don't need to memorize these names.
- Every protein has this level.

> **Analogy:** Level 3 is like a **sentence**. Words come together into one complete thought.

> 📷 See **Slide 6** — each of the four pulls is labeled in pink where two side chains meet.

---

## 7. The hydrophobic effect 🎯

**In water, a chain folds so its water-fearing parts hide in the middle.**

- **Hydrophobic** means "water-fearing." Hydrophobic things don't mix with water *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*.
- **Nonpolar** amino acids are hydrophobic. **Polar** amino acids mix with water.
- When the chain folds, nonpolar amino acids gather in the **center**. Polar amino acids stay on the **outside**.
- Folding shrinks the **hydration layer**, the layer of water around the chain.

> **Analogy:** Oil drops in water clump together. Nonpolar amino acids do the same.

> 📷 See **Slide 8** — red dots (nonpolar) end up in the middle. Yellow dots (polar) stay on the edge. Slide 7 shows the chain before folding.

---

## 8. Level 4 — Quaternary structure 🎯

**Quaternary structure is two or more chains fitting together into one protein.**

- **What it is:** **2 or more subunits** packed together.
- A **subunit** is one polypeptide chain inside a bigger protein.
- **Key fact:** NOT ALL PROTEINS HAVE THIS! A protein with only one chain stops at Level 3.

> **Analogy:** Level 4 is like a **paragraph**. Several sentences work together, like several chains.

> 📷 See **Slide 9** — one folded chain, then an arrow, then several chains packed into one protein.

---

## 9. Summary: the alphabet analogy 🎯

**Slide 10 matches each level of a protein to a part of writing.**

| Level | What it is | Like… |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | The order of amino acids | Letters |
| **Secondary** | Coils and zigzag sheets | Words |
| **Tertiary** | The whole 3D shape of one chain | Sentences |
| **Quaternary** | 2 or more chains together (not all proteins) | Paragraphs |

- Each level is built from the one before it.

> 📷 See **Slide 10** — pink arrows link each writing box to its protein level. (The slide spells it "Quanternary.")

## 10. Nucleic acids 🎯

**Nucleic acids hold the information passed down from parents.**

- **Jobs:** they store and pass on **hereditary information**, the information passed from parents to offspring. They also hold the code for making proteins.
- **Examples:** DNA and RNA.
- The monomer of nucleic acids is the **nucleotide**.
- Each nucleotide has three parts: a **5-carbon sugar**, a **phosphate group**, and a **nitrogenous base**. The base is the "letter" part: A, T, C, G, or U.

> **Why:** This links back to Level 1. A gene's DNA sets the order of amino acids in a protein.

> 📷 See **Slide 11** — the picture on the right labels one nucleotide: phosphate, ribose (a sugar), and base.

---

## 11. DNA vs. RNA 🎯

**DNA and RNA share three bases, but each has one base of its own.**

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| **Full name** | Deoxyribonucleic acid | Ribonucleic acid |
| **Bases** | A, **T**, C, G | A, **U**, C, G |
| **Sugar** | Deoxyribose | Ribose |

- DNA uses **T**, which stands for **thymine**.
- RNA uses **U**, which stands for **uracil**, in place of T.
- Both use A, C, and G.

> 📷 See **Slide 14** — thymine goes to DNA only, and uracil goes to RNA only. A, C, and G go to both.

---

## 12. DNA structure: the double helix 🎯

**DNA is a double helix: two strands twisted around each other.**

- **Outside:** sugars and phosphates form the **sugar-phosphate backbone**.
- **Inside:** the bases pair up, like the steps of a staircase. **Hydrogen bonds** hold each pair together.
- **Pairing rule:** A pairs with T. C pairs with G.
- The strands are **antiparallel**. They run in opposite directions. This matters when DNA is copied.

> **Analogy:** DNA is like a twisted ladder. The backbones are the rails. The base pairs are the rungs.

> 📷 See **Slide 12** — P circles and sugars sit outside. A–T and C–G pairs sit in the middle. Slide 13 shows more drawings of the same ideas.

---

## Quick review

- Proteins are made of amino acids. Nucleic acids are made of nucleotides.
- STRUCTURE = FUNCTION: a protein's shape decides its job.
- A peptide is about 2–50 amino acids. A polypeptide is about 51 or more.
- The four levels match letters, words, sentences, and paragraphs.
- Peptide bonds join Level 1. Hydrogen bonds hold Level 2. Side chains shape Level 3.
- Not all proteins have Level 4, quaternary structure.
- DNA uses A, T, C, G. RNA uses A, U, C, G.
- In DNA, A pairs with T, and C pairs with G.
