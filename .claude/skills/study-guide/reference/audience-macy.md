# Writing for Macy (high school biology)

Macy needs extra help learning the content, and it is very important that she is not overwhelmed.
Everything she sees must be very easy to understand and friendly: study guide, outline, flashcards,
quiz, scenarios, glossary, maps, slide notes, cheat sheet, and audio summary.

**Easy to read never means leaving things out.** Make it simple by how you write and organize it,
not by cutting.

These rules sit on top of the normal study-guide rules (source-only content, citations for outside
facts, no filler, no emoji in app strings).

## Two hard rules (user, 2026-09-12)

1. **NEVER guess what is or isn't on a test, or what she does or doesn't need to learn.** If she
   thinks something is optional, she will skip it. Unless the teacher's own words (slides or
   speaker notes) say so:
   - no 🎯 / ⛔ flags, no "core" vs. "extra detail", no "don't memorize", no "background only",
     no "Exam scope" guesses, no "On the test" lists.
   - Treat everything in the deck as something to learn.
   - When the teacher DOES say something about scope, quote it exactly and attribute it
     ("Your teacher's note on Slide 7: …"). Don't turn it into "skip this".
2. **NEVER cut material from the deck** unless it is already covered somewhere else in the guide.
   Every slide's text, every speaker note, and every label or fact visible in a slide picture must
   appear in the study guide (and in short form in the outline). Clarifications of confusing slide
   wording belong in Source Notes. Keep them all, but write them simply.

3. **NEVER add content that isn't from the teacher** (slides, speaker notes, slide pictures). No new
   facts, terms, examples of the science, or extra members of a list the slide didn't give (e.g. the
   slide labels cytosine and thymine as pyrimidines — don't add uracil). The only allowed addition is
   a plain explanation of a word or idea the teacher already uses, when she'd need it to understand.
4. **Every outside-source addition is labeled up front**, so she always knows it isn't the
   teacher's: start it with **"Outside source:"**, then the plain explanation and an everyday
   example, then the citation. Example: "**Outside source:** Blubber is a thick layer of fat under a
   seal's skin. Like a winter coat, it keeps heat in. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*". In the glossary, put
   the teacher's wording first; any outside explanation follows as "Outside source: …". Keep app
   strings emoji-free (the words "Outside source:" are fine there).

## Voice
- **Plain language, always** (plainlanguage.gov style): common words, active voice, main point first.
  If a 9th grader who struggles with reading would stumble on a word, give its plain meaning.
- Talk to her directly ("you"). Warm, calm, encouraging. Never babyish, never jokey filler.
- **Short sentences: aim for 8–12 words, never over 20.** One idea per sentence.
- Every science word gets a plain meaning right next to it the first time: **cohesion** (water
  sticking to water). Never two new terms in one sentence; split into separate bullets.

## Shape of each study-guide section
Each slide-topic gets one section, in lesson order:
1. `## N. Topic name` (plain heading, no flag emoji).
2. **One bold sentence that says the main idea.**
3. Short bullets with the facts. Use as many as the slide needs; one fact per bullet.
4. If a slide has a lot, group bullets under small bold lead-ins ("**What it is:**",
   "**In the picture:**", "**Numbers on the slide:**") so it stays easy to scan. No nested bullets.
5. Optional: one `> **Analogy:**` or `> **Why:**` callout.
6. `> 📷 See **Slide N** — …` saying what to look at in the picture.

Tables only when they make it simpler (max 3–4 columns). Keep sections chunked so nothing feels
like a wall of text.

Top of the guide: the header lines, then **"What you'll learn"** (one line per section), then the
sections, then **"Quick review"** (one-line reminders of every section), then **"Class discussion"**
if the deck has partner-talk or BONUS prompts.

Class discussion: list each prompt exactly as the teacher wrote it. The slides don't give answers.
- If the slide's own picture shows the answer, say so: "**Answer (from the Slide N picture):** …".
- Otherwise label it **"Possible answer:"** and never present it as the teacher's answer.
- **Any answer or fact from an outside source** (user, 2026-09-12) gets an easy-to-understand
  explanation in two or three short sentences **plus one or two everyday examples**, then the
  citation. Example: "**Possible answer:** Seals and whales. They have a thick layer of fat called
  blubber under their skin. Like a winter coat, it keeps their body heat in. *(OpenStax Biology 2e)*"
- The same rule applies to outside-source facts anywhere else in the guide or Source Notes.

Test scope: at the end of the guide, one short paragraph: "Your teacher hasn't shared a test list
for this lesson, so study everything here." Add any exact teacher quotes about scope. Nothing else.

## Lesson outline
The quick-scan version: one `##` per slide-topic with short one-line bullets covering every fact.

## Glossary
Every term that appears in the lesson, including terms only shown in slide pictures. Definition =
one short plain sentence (≤ 15 words).

## Practice items stand on their own (user, 2026-09-13)
Flashcards, quiz, scenarios, mnemonics, concept maps, glossary, audio summary, and cheat sheet
must make sense with nothing else open. **Never mention slides, slide numbers, pictures,
drawings, charts, photos, "the notes", the deck, or green boxes** in them. Ask about the idea
itself: not "What is the brown wall labeled in the Slide 4 picture?" but "What part of a plant
do water molecules stick to by adhesion?" Teacher quotes are fine as "Your teacher says…".
Slide references belong only in the study guide, outline, source notes, and the slide gallery.

## Flashcards
- Cover every section and every fact a quiz could ask about. Count follows the content
  (typically 25–45); order them the same way as the guide so they build up.
- Question: short and direct, one fact. Answer: **1–6 words** or a very short list (max 4 items).

## Quiz
- About one question per section, plus more for big sections (typically 12–20).
- **Ask direct questions** ("What is…?", "Which…?"). No fill-in-the-blank stems ending in "…".
- 3–4 options that are clearly different ideas from elsewhere in the lesson. No trick wording,
  no "all/none of the above", no double negatives.
- **No option that is just the right answer flipped or one letter off** ("less dense" vs "more
  dense", T-A-C-G vs U-A-C-G).

## Scenarios
- **4–6** everyday situations she can picture (kitchen, weather, sports, plants, food).
- Stem ≤ 2 sentences and ends with a question. Rationale ≤ 2 short sentences naming the idea.

## Mnemonics, maps, notes
- Mnemonics: only ones that really help.
- Concept maps: small (≤ 6 nodes each), every arrow labeled with plain words.
- SLIDE_NOTES: one or two short sentences per slide, plain words, no "skip/extra" language. No
  `star` flags — `profile-macy.json` removes the "key image" stars and filter, since ranking slides
  by importance is a guess about what matters.
- SUMMARY (audio): ~150–250 words, slow and simple, like a friendly tutor recap.
- printCheat: covers every section in short lines (may run past one printed page).

## Source notes (transcript-corrections.md)
Short and reassuring. Say there's no recording. List slide typos in a small table. For any slide
wording that is simplified or could confuse her, give a one- or two-sentence clearer version with
a citation. Keep every one of these; write them simply.

## The app's Overview
- The "On the test" heading is renamed "Topics in this lesson" by `profile-macy.json`; TESTCHIPS
  lists every section.
- swaps.json: `notTestedHeading` = "From your teacher"; `notTestedHtml` = a
  `<p class="small muted">` that quotes any teacher note about scope, or says "Your teacher hasn't
  shared a test list for this lesson, so study every topic."
