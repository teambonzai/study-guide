# Writing for Macy (high school biology)

Macy needs extra help learning the content, and **it is very important that she is not
overwhelmed.** Everything she sees — study guide, outline, flashcards, quiz, scenarios, glossary,
maps, slide notes, cheat sheet, audio summary — must be very easy to understand and friendly.
When in doubt, cut. A shorter guide she finishes beats a complete one she abandons.

These rules sit on top of the normal study-guide rules (source-only content, citations for outside
facts, deck-emphasis flags, no filler, no emoji in app strings).

## Voice
- **Plain language, always** (plainlanguage.gov style): common words, active voice, the main
  point first. If a 9th grader who struggles with reading would stumble on a word, replace it.
- Talk to her directly ("you"). Warm, calm, encouraging — never babyish, never jokey filler.
- **Short sentences: aim for 8–12 words, never over 20.** One idea per sentence.
- Everyday words. Say "sticks to" before "adheres." When a science word is needed, give it once
  with a plain meaning right next to it: **cohesion** (water sticking to water).
- No stacked jargon. Never two new terms in one sentence.
- No long parentheticals, no "precisely", no edge cases or exceptions unless the slide teaches them.
- Numbers only when the slide makes them the point.

## Shape of each study-guide section
Each slide-topic gets ONE short section, in this order:
1. `## N. Topic name 🎯` (plain heading, lesson order).
2. **One bold sentence that says the main idea.**
3. 2–4 short bullets — the must-know facts, nothing more.
4. At most ONE callout: an `> **Analogy:**` from everyday life OR a `> **Why:**` — not both.
5. `> 📷 See **Slide N** — …` one line saying what to look at in the picture.

Hard limits: no section over ~120 words. Tables only when they truly make it simpler (max 3
columns, max 5 rows) — e.g. a side-by-side compare. No nested bullets.

Top of the guide: the header lines, then a short **"What you'll learn"** list (3–6 bullets, one line
each), then the sections, then **"Quick review"** (5–8 one-line must-knows), then Exam scope last.

Exam scope: 2–3 short sentences: there's no teacher test list; ✅ items are the core ideas from the
slides; ⛔ items are extra details you don't need to memorize. Then a short ⛔ list.

Class discussion / BONUS prompts: one line each with a one-line answer, under "Class discussion"
near the end. Skip entirely if the deck has none.

## Lesson outline
The quick-scan version: one `##` per slide-topic with 1–3 one-line bullets. No callouts.

## Glossary
Only terms she needs for this lesson (typically 12–25). Definition = one short plain sentence
(≤ 15 words). Drop background-only terms.

## Flashcards
- Core ideas only. Typical lesson: **15–25 cards** (fewer for short decks).
- Question: short and direct, one fact. Answer: **1–6 words** or a very short list (max 3 items).
- Build up: simple "what is" cards first, then "which one / why" cards.

## Quiz
- **10–15 questions.** Short stems. 3–4 options that are clearly different — no trick wording,
  no "all/none of the above", no double negatives.
- Wrong options should be plausible but not confusingly close.
- **Ask direct questions** ("What is…?", "Which…?") — no fill-in-the-blank stems ending in "…".
- **No option that is just the right answer flipped or one letter off** ("less dense" vs "more
  dense", "slowly" vs "quickly", T-A-C-G vs U-A-C-G). Use wrong answers that are clearly different
  ideas from elsewhere in the lesson.
- Every scenario stem ends with a question.
- Don't list several new term names in one sentence; if the slide names them but they aren't core,
  say what they do in plain words and mark the names ⛔.

## Scenarios
- **4–5** everyday situations she can picture (kitchen, weather, sports, plants, food).
- Stem ≤ 2 sentences. Rationale ≤ 2 short sentences that name the idea.

## Mnemonics, maps, notes
- Mnemonics: 2–4, only ones that really help.
- Concept maps: 2–3, each small (≤ 6 nodes), every arrow labeled with plain words.
- SLIDE_NOTES: one short sentence (≤ 20 words) per slide, plain words.
- SUMMARY (audio): ~120–180 words, slow and simple, like a friendly tutor recap.
- printCheat: one page, big ideas only, short lines.

## Source notes (transcript-corrections.md)
Keep it short and reassuring. Say there's no recording. List slide typos in one small table. For
any slide statement that is simplified, give a one-sentence clearer version with a citation — only
if it could actually confuse her. Skip fine print.
